Agents

FlashWatch's forecasts and dollar figures come from its own models. Getting them to a farmer, and into the farm's equipment, is the agents' job. Each one below runs on a Meta model, works through tools that return FlashWatch's numbers, and is checked before anything reaches the farmer or a pivot. These are real runs, replayed on past seasons.

Finds your pivots

SAM 3.1

Setting up a farm shouldn't mean typing coordinates. Given the farm's location, FlashWatch fetches a USDA aerial photo, asks SAM 3.1 to find every centre-pivot circle, and turns each one into a pivot: where it is, how wide, how many acres.

Plans the morning, runs the pivots

Muse Spark

For a farm with several pivots, the operator agent reads each pivot's advice, its water account and the farm's limits, proposes which pivots to start, and asks the farmer by text. Fixed farm rules check every command; nothing moves until the farmer replies YES.

Watches the forecast for you

Muse Spark

Most days need no message. The watcher checks each farm on every forecast and texts only when something changes: the advice turns to start, a decision's window opens with the odds in favour, the pivot can't keep up with the crop, or drought sets in. Listens to the field

Muse Voice TranscribeMuse Spark

A farmer leaves a voice note from the field. Muse Voice Transcribe turns it into text; Muse Spark matches what was said to the farm's pivots and rates the crop stress. The operator agent treats a farmer-reported stressed crop as a reason to water.

Writes the weekly briefing

Muse Spark

For a county or extension office: risk this week, why, and what to watch. The agent gathers numbers through tools; a checker traces every figure in the draft back to a tool result and sends the draft back if one can't be traced.

Writes like the Drought Monitor

Muse Glimmer 30B, open weights

The US Drought Monitor's weekly narrative is the text farmers and agencies already read. FlashWatch fine-tunes Meta's open-weights Muse Glimmer on 15 years of those narratives, paired with the week's numbers, so it can write the same kind of summary from FlashWatch's data, a week or more ahead of the Monitor.

The safeguards

  • Numbers come from tools. Agents never make up a figure; a checker compares every number in a draft with the tool results and sends it back if one doesn't match.
  • Farm rules are code, not prompts. Well capacity, water allocation, season and the advice are checked by fixed rules that the agent can't talk its way past. Refusals go back to the agent with the reason.
  • The farmer approves. Commands reach a pivot only after a YES. Pivots connect through a signed webhook, Lindsay FieldNET's data through Leaf, or a to-do in the maker's app.
