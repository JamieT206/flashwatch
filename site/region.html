FlashWatch gives farmers advance warning of flash-drought conditions and advises them on supplemental irrigation strategy.

A flash drought can dry a field out in two or three weeks, usually in a summer hot spell. FlashWatch combines ECMWF's weather forecasts with ERA5-Land soil moisture (satellite and weather-station data, blended by the EU's Copernicus service) in a machine-learning model that gives the chance of a flash drought at each farm one and two weeks ahead. A water-balance model of the field, with USDA crop prices and county yields, turns that chance into dollars: start extra irrigation today, or wait. AI agents built on Meta's Muse models send the advice by text each morning and, once the farmer replies YES, pass it to their farm-management and pivot-control software. FlashWatch sees a flash drought coming a median three weeks before the US Drought Monitor marks it.
